1 · Feature overview

The hard problem isn't translation – it's judgment

Global campaigns are either expensive to recreate by hand per market, or awkwardly translated in place. Knowing which parts of a creative idea are invariant and which need to be culturally rebuilt is the actual, unsolved problem.

What it is

An AI feature that scans a finished ad, proposes what's locked (identical everywhere) vs. local (actor, voice, music, humor, pacing, on-screen text), then generates specific, reasoned edits per market – never a silent blanket translation.

Who it's for

Global marketing teams and agencies shipping one campaign to many markets at once – an enterprise/agency audience, not a solo creator producing one piece at a time.

Why Artlist

Artlist already owns the raw capability competitors use piecemeal – dubbing, lip-sync, AI avatars, and a licensed, culturally-broad music catalog, all in one platform. The gap isn't infrastructure, it's the judgment layer deciding what to use it for, per market.

Competitive gap

HeyGen, Perso, ElevenLabs, Arcads and WPP Open solve the mechanical "how" – voice cloning, lip-sync, avatar swaps. None solve the "what and why": deciding, with visible reasoning, what actually needs to change for a given market.

Keep or localize

The AI scans the master cut once – brand assets, spoken language, music licensing, visible text – and tags every element Keep or Localize, with a stated reason. The user can override any call before it fans out to every market.

3 · UI exploration

Deliberately not Artlist's own system

Both screens break from Artlist's existing dark, restrained interface on purpose – a bold, maximalist, editorial language instead: hard grid lines, oversized italic display type, saturated color-blocking, thick strokes.

Why loud, for a trust feature. The whole point of this flow is making AI judgment legible – a muted dashboard would bury exactly the decisions that need to stand out: what's locked, what's flagged, what's confirmed.
Where it stays calm on purpose. Inside the bold shell, the actual decision points – toggles, tables, confirm states – use restrained color logic (one accent per state) so the loud system never undercuts trust in the data itself.
One continuous language, not two mockups. Country chips, the Keep/Localize split, and the per-market confirm flow all reuse the same type, color and border logic – the two screens read as one product, not two disconnected concepts.
Palette
Pink / accent Indigo / surface Yellow / action Light blue / state Ink / text

Four colors, each with one job – accent, surface, the single call-to-action color, and one state indicator. Nothing else competes with those four.

4 · Reasoning & decisions

Where trust in the AI is actually built

Every other step in this flow is either setup (Source, Markets) or output (Results). Breakdown and Suggestions are the two moments a producer has to decide whether to believe what the AI proposed – first about what's even in scope, then about whether a specific execution is good enough to ship.

Breakdown answers the scope question

"Can this element change at all, in any market?" Decided once, globally – a brand or legal call, not a creative one.

Suggestions answers the execution question

"Is this specific localized version right for this specific market?" Decided repeatedly, market by market – a creative call, not a policy one.

Alternatives considered

Cultural localization was one of five directions weighed against Artlist's actual gaps – adaptive edit-following music, persistent brand/style memory, automatic scene-aware sound design, and simulated pre-publish audience testing were the others. Localization won because it most directly uses a combination Artlist already owns and competitors don't hold in one place – dubbing, lip-sync, avatars and a licensed catalog – rather than proposing new infrastructure.

Key trade-off: a global, once-decided Breakdown instead of re-litigating Keep/Localize inside every market screen. Faster, and keeps brand-protected elements out of the per-market pipeline entirely – at the cost of needing a clear override path for the rare market that genuinely needs an exception to a locked element, which is noted as a next step rather than solved here.
